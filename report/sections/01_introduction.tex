\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Smartwatches are worn continuously throughout the day and record fine-grained wrist motion through built-in inertial measurement units (IMUs) with accelerometer and gyroscope. Designed for step counting and fitness tracking, these sensors capture far richer information about the wearer's activity. One largely unexplored application is the automatic detection of handwriting activity: determining whether a person is currently writing by hand based solely on the motion of their wrist.

Handwriting remains a fundamental activity in education, professional life, and personal note-taking. Detecting it passively, without the user starting a timer or touching the device, enables tracking productive writing time, monitoring engagement in educational settings, and building environments that adapt to whether a person is composing thoughts on paper.

Prior work on writing detection from wrist-worn sensors has focused on recognising \textit{what} is written, such as letters, words, or gestures, rather than the simpler but practically more useful question of \textit{whether} writing is happening at all \cite{soubam2023, xiamotionhacker2018}. These approaches often rely on controlled laboratory conditions, few participants, and evaluation strategies that do not reflect how well a model generalises to unseen users.

In this work, we address these limitations. We collect IMU data from an Apple Watch worn by 32 participants during a counterbalanced study protocol that includes genuine writing conditions as well as hard negative activities such as keyboard typing, phone typing, scrolling, pen-fidgeting, and gesturing. Ground-truth writing labels come from a Moleskine smart pen during data collection only; once trained, the model runs on the watch alone. All models are evaluated with a grouped five-fold cross-validation over participants, so that no participant is ever in their own training set, the condition that matters in a real deployment.

Our central research question is:

\begin{quote}
\textit{Can writing activity be reliably detected from the wrist IMU of an Apple Watch alone, independently of who is wearing the watch and what they write?}
\end{quote}

We compare a Random Forest on 88 hand-engineered features, a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) post-filter, and deep temporal models (TCN, TCN with a bidirectional GRU head).

Section~\ref{sec:data-collection} describes the data collection, Section~\ref{sec:methodology} the processing and models, and Section~\ref{sec:experimental-setup} the evaluation protocol.
